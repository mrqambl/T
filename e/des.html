🌍 Development – Baseline Revision Pack

Grade 5 friendly • Simple explanations • Key terms • Practice questions

Big idea: Development means improvements in people’s quality of life, their economy, and their standard of living.

1. Key Terms You Must Know

Development: Improvements in people’s quality of life, economy and standard of living.
Quality of life: How healthy, safe, comfortable and happy people are.
Standard of living: How much people can afford and the quality of things they have.
Economy: The system of producing, buying and selling goods and services.
Indicator: A measurement used to show how developed a place is.
LIC: Low Income Country — a country with a low average income.
MIC: Middle Income Country — a country with a middle average income.
HIC: High Income Country — a country with a high average income.
HDI: Human Development Index — a measure that combines health, education and income to show a country’s level of human development.
GNI per person: The average income earned by people in a country, including income from abroad.
GDP per person: The average value of goods and services produced per person in a country.

3. Development Indicators

Indicators are facts or numbers that help us compare development between places.

IndicatorSimple definitionWhat usually shows higher development?
Life expectancyThe average number of years a person is expected to live.Higher life expectancy
Literacy rateThe percentage of people who can read and write.Higher literacy rate
Infant mortality rateThe number of babies who die before age one, usually measured per 1,000 live births.Lower infant mortality
GNI/GDP per personAverage income or production per person.Higher income/production
Access to clean waterThe percentage of people who can get safe, clean water.Greater access
Years of schoolingThe average number of years people spend in education.More years
Quick recall: More development usually means higher life expectancy, literacy, income, clean-water access and schooling, but lower infant mortality.

4. Human Development Index (HDI)

HDI measures human development using three main areas:
  • Health – mainly life expectancy.
  • Education – years of schooling.
  • Income – income per person.
A higher HDI generally means people have better opportunities and living conditions.

5. Global Pattern of Development

Development is uneven around the world. Some places have much higher levels of development than others.

Highest: High levels of development. Many HICs, such as Norway, have very high HDI.
Lowest: Lower levels of development. Some LICs have low income and poorer access to healthcare and education.
Overall trend: Development is generally higher in many parts of Europe, North America, Australia and New Zealand, while many lower-development countries are found in sub-Saharan Africa. There are important differences within and between regions.
Anomaly: A place that does not fit the general pattern. For example, Singapore is a small, highly developed country in Southeast Asia, while nearby countries have different development levels.
Useful geography words: highest • lowest • overall trend • pattern • anomaly • compare • contrast • region

6. Factors Affecting Development

Development is affected by many factors. Learn the four main groups plus history.

🌦 Physical Factors

FactorSimple explanationHow it can affect development
ClimateThe usual weather of a place.Extreme drought or floods can damage crops, homes and jobs.
Natural hazardsEvents such as earthquakes, floods, storms and volcanoes.They can destroy buildings, roads, farms and businesses.
Natural resourcesUseful materials from nature, such as oil, gas, minerals and fertile soil.Resources can provide jobs, exports and government income.
LandlockedA country with no coastline.It may have higher transport costs because goods must cross other countries to reach ports.

💰 Economic Factors

FactorSimple explanationEffect
TradeBuying and selling goods and services between places.Successful trade can bring money, jobs and investment.
DebtMoney a country owes.Large debt repayments can reduce money available for schools, hospitals and infrastructure.
EmploymentHaving paid work.More jobs can increase incomes and tax money.
InvestmentSpending money now to improve or grow something.Investment can create businesses, jobs, roads, schools and services.

🏫 Social Factors

FactorSimple explanationEffect
EducationLearning and gaining skills.Education can improve skills, jobs and incomes.
HealthcareServices that prevent and treat illness.Good healthcare can reduce deaths and improve life expectancy.
Population growthHow quickly the number of people increases.Fast growth can put pressure on schools, jobs, housing, food and water.

🏛 Political Factors

FactorSimple explanationEffect
ConflictSerious fighting between groups or countries.It can destroy homes, schools, roads and businesses and force people to move.
Government stabilityHow steady and effective a government is.Stable governments can plan and provide services more effectively.
CorruptionUsing public power or money dishonestly for personal benefit.It can waste money and reduce trust and investment.

📜 Historical Factors

Colonialism: When a powerful country controlled another territory. Colonial rule sometimes focused on taking resources and shaping economies for the benefit of the colonial power.
Past exploitation: When people or resources were unfairly used for another group's benefit. Past exploitation can leave countries with economic and social disadvantages.

8. How to Answer an 8-Mark Question

Question: “With reference to examples, explain the factors affecting development.”

Use this simple chain:

FACTOR → HOW IT AFFECTS DEVELOPMENT → EXAMPLE → IMPACT ON PEOPLE/ECONOMY

Example paragraph

Factor: Conflict can limit development. How? Fighting can destroy roads, schools, hospitals and businesses, making it harder for people to work and receive services. Example: Afghanistan has experienced long periods of conflict. Impact: Damage to infrastructure and disruption to services can reduce quality of life and make economic development more difficult.

Another example

Factor: Education can increase development. How? Education gives people skills that can help them get better jobs. Example: South Korea invested heavily in education as it developed. Impact: A more skilled workforce can support businesses, productivity and higher incomes.

8-mark secret: Do not just list factors. Always explain WHY each factor changes development.
