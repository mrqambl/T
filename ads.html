Unit 3: QAR (Question-Answer Relationships) Masterclass

QAR teaches you where to find the answers to reading comprehension questions. Master the four categories:

In the Book

Right There

What is it? The answer is directly in the text. The words used in the question and the words used for the answer are right in the same sentence.

How to find it? Look for key words from the question directly in the passage. You can put your finger right on it.

Example Question: Who was sitting next to Pedro?

Answer: His mom was sitting next to him. (Found in: "Pedro grabbed his mom’s hand.")

In the Book

Think and Search

What is it? The answer is in the text, but you need to look in more than one place or think about context clues (like finding a synonym or summary).

How to find it? Search across multiple sentences, connect ideas, or find a vocabulary match from clues.

Example Question: Find a word in the passage that means "snatched".

Answer: "Grabbed" (You have to search the sentences and connect the meaning of snatched to grabbed).

In My Head

Author and Me

What is it? The answer is not directly stated in the text. You must combine clues from the author with your own life experience and background knowledge.

How to find it? Ask yourself: What does the author tell me, and what do I already know about this feeling or situation?

Example Question: Why do you think Pedro smiled at the end of the flight?

Answer: Because he overcame his fear and realized that flying was fun and exciting.

In My Head

On My Own

What is it? You can answer the question without even reading the story! It asks about your own thoughts, life experiences, or general knowledge.

How to find it? Look into your own mind and feelings. The text is not needed to answer the question.

Example Question: How would you feel if you had to ride an airplane for the very first time?

Answer: I would feel excited and a little nervous to look out the small window.

Unit 4: Grammar, Verb Tenses & Word Anatomy

Master verbs, simple present tense rules, present continuous (-ing), prefixes, suffixes, base words, and spatial prepositions.

Lesson 1: Verbs and the Simple Present Tense

Concept: A verb is a word that shows an action, occurrence, or state of being. We change the verb to show when something happens.

Key Rule: In the Simple Present Tense, if the subject is singular (he, she, it, singular noun), we add "-s" or "-es" to the base verb. If the subject is plural (they, we, you, I), we keep the base verb.

Correct Usage Common Error to Avoid Rule Explanation
He runs to the park every day. He run to the park everyday. Add "s" because "He" is singular.
The girls read books after school. The girls reads books after school. "The girls" is plural, so use base verb "read".
Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius. — Used for scientific facts and habitual actions.

Lesson 2: The Present Continuous Tense

Concept: We use the Present Continuous Tense when an action is happening right now, at the exact moment of speaking.

Key Rule: Formed by: Subject + am/is/are + verb ending in "-ing". (e.g., I am running, He is studying, They are jumping).

Correct Usage Common Error to Avoid Rule Explanation
I am jumping right now. I am jump right now. Must add "-ing" to the main verb after "am".
He is studying for his test. He is study. "is study" is incorrect; use "is studying".
The birds are singing in the garden. — Happening in the present moment.

Lesson 3: Word Anatomy – Prefixes, Suffixes & Base Words

Concept: A root or base word is the simplest form of a word. A prefix is added to the beginning, and a suffix is added to the end to alter its meaning or tense.

Key Rule: Prefixes change meaning (e.g., mis- = wrongly, sub- = under, un- = not). Suffixes change tense or part of speech (e.g., -ed = past tense, -ing = continuous, -ly = adverb).

Correct Usage Common Error to Avoid Rule Explanation
misinterpreted = mis (prefix) + interpret (base word) + ed (suffix) — Base word is "interpret".
submarine = sub (prefix meaning under) + marine (relating to sea) — Prefix sub- means under or beneath.
talked = talk (base) + ed (suffix showing past tense) — Suffix -ed signals past.

Lesson 4: Prepositions of Place and Time

Concept: Prepositions are linking words that show relationships of position, direction, or time between nouns and other words.

Key Rule: Prepositions of place show location: in (inside a space), on (on a surface), under (beneath), beside (next to). For holidays and specific calendar days, we use "on" (e.g., "on Qatar National Day").

Correct Usage Common Error to Avoid Rule Explanation
The boy is in his house. The boy is on his house. People stay inside (in) the house, not sitting on the roof.
We celebrate on Qatar National Day. We celebrate Qatar National Day at. Use "on" for specific calendar dates and national days.
The giraffe is beside the box. — Beside means positioned at the side of.
